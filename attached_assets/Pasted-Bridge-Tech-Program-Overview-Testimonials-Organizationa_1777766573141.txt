Bridge Tech Program Overview & Testimonials
Organizational Structure and Sponsorship
Bridge Tech is in the process of acquiring official 501(c)(3) nonprofit status. While we initially operated under the umbrella of a fiscal sponsorship through Connect For Purpose (a California-based nonprofit), we have since raised funds independently and are now covering all paperwork and legal costs ourselves. This milestone allows us to operate as a fully independent nonprofit organization dedicated to empowering youth through technology education.

Second Program Overview
Total Students Registered: 40
 Program Sections:

Our second program expanded in both size and scope. We adjusted lesson content based on age group, allowing us to better cater to students' developmental stages and prior experience. The engagement and enthusiasm continued to grow, with many returning students and new families joining the Bridge Tech community.
We also recognized the need for students to have consistent access to technology. Many did not have personal devices, which prompted a fundraising initiative.

Bake Sale Fundraiser
To support our students and sustain our operations, we organized a community bake sale, which became a beautiful example of grassroots fundraising and teamwork.
Hours Baking: 10


Hours at Sale Booth: 4


Items Sold: The majority of baked goods


Funds Raised: $2,000


Fund Allocation:
$1,000 for 5 Chromebooks (helping students without access to tech)


$1,000 for 501(c)(3) paperwork (covering all legal and filing costs)


Because of this fundraiser, we are no longer relying on fiscal sponsorship—we’re officially investing in our future as an independent nonprofit organization.

Closing Note
Bridge Tech is more than a coding class—it’s a growing community of Muslim youth, mentors, and families who believe in the power of education, creativity, and collaboration. From Scratch projects to Python games, from web design to real-world AI discussions, our students have shown curiosity, resilience, and joy in learning.
With every new class, bake sale, and line of code written, we are building a future where our youth are not just consumers of technology—but creators, leaders, and changemakers.
